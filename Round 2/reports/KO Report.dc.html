1 · Investment thesis

At USD 94.08, with a cost of equity of 6.68%, Coca-Cola is priced for 4.4% dividend growth forever, against about 4% at the last raise [2]. The business is doing well: Q3 volume rose 5%, organic revenue 7%, and guidance went up to at least 11% comparable EPS growth [1]. But the market has paid for this. The stock is close to its 52-week high at 26.7x forward earnings, against 17.6x for peers, while consensus EPS growth slows to 5.6% next year and the bottler sales take about 2% off 2027 EPS [1]. This is an excellent company at a full price, not a mispricing we can buy. The two-stage DDM gives USD 67.23 and the comparables USD 59.13, a margin of safety of −29%. Alpha is negative even if the gap never closes, so we would not hold it in a long-only book.

3 · Valuation: dividend discount model
Why this method

Coca-Cola has raised its dividend for 64 years [2] and returns cash mainly through it: USD 6.8bn of dividends against USD 0.9bn of buybacks in nine months [4]. We use two stages because earnings are growing faster than a global staple can sustain forever: 6.0% for 5 years, near consensus of 5.6%, then 3.0%.

Methods that do not fit

Gordon alone would apply one rate to both phases. A cash-flow DCF would start from FY25 FCF, cut by the one-off fairlife payment [6]. P/B says little, as brands built in-house are not on the balance sheet. The comparables are a second opinion only: few peers have a concentrate model with these margins, so some premium is deserved.

7 · Specific issues
Value trap

Not a trap: FY25 payout/FCF of 1.80 reflects the USD 6.1bn fairlife payment. Without it FCF was USD 11.4bn [6], and FY26 guidance is at least USD 13.0bn [1].

Sustainable growth

Forward ROE 40.6% × 40% retained = 16.4%, far above g2 of 3.0%. But ROE reflects thin book equity (P/B 10.83x), so new capital would not earn it.

Management quality

CEO Henrique Braun, since 31 Mar 2026, joined in 1996 [7]. Capital goes to dividends and debt: USD 0.9bn of buybacks in nine months, USD 6.8bn of dividends [4].

Activist investment

No route: a stake is very costly at about USD 400bn of market cap, and Berkshire, with 400m shares [10], has held since 1988 [8]. We found no activist filing.

Upside potential

A g2 of about 4.2% would justify the price, close to the 4.4% it implies. A court win returns USD 6.6bn [4]. Analyst mean target: USD 97.75 (+4%).

Risks: what would prove us wrong

We are wrong if Q3's volume growth lasts and dividends compound above 4.4%, or if long yields fall. Low beta already helps: at beta 1 the DDM is USD 30.27.

Model flags: Negative alpha: weight 0 (long-only).
