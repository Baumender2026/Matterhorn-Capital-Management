Margin of safety and alpha

Margin of safety +9.2%, alpha +10.3% a year (IR 0.23), assuming the gap closes within one year.

Catalyst

Q3 FY2027 results and Q4 guidance · 17 Nov (company). Set by the company for 17 Nov, after the close [2]; the quarter ended on 25 Oct. A quarter at or above the USD 108bn guide, with Rubin in full production [1], would show that demand is not slowing and move the price towards fair value.

Decision

Long candidate. We buy, although the margin of +9% is below our 25% rule: the comparables (USD 254.31) and analysts (USD 327.14) see more, and a dated report can close the gap. Its high residual risk keeps the Treynor-Black weight small.

2 · Valuation: dividend discount model
Why this method

NVIDIA returns cash mostly through buybacks: USD 39.0bn in the first half against USD 6.3bn of dividends [1]. So we discount dividends plus buybacks (cheat sheet p. 2). Two stages, because growth is far above what any company can keep forever: 40.0% for 8 years, then 3.0%.

Methods that do not fit

Gordon alone gives USD 24.98: one rate cannot fit a company growing this fast. P/B (19.68x) says little, as its value is in chip designs and software, not on the balance sheet. Peers are smaller and differently built, so the comparables are only a double check.

3.1 · The expected information ratio and its credibility

The active portfolio's IR is √Σ(αi/σ(εi))² = 2.43: at wA = 21.9% it lifts the ex-ante Sharpe Ratio of the SAA from 0.30 to 1.07. We do not find it credible. By the Fundamental Law, IR = IC × √N: with N = 3 it needs an IC of 1.4, where 0.05–0.10 is good skill. Every alpha assumes the gap closes within a year, PEP and ABBV are above Buffett's 0.64, and only PEP clears our 25% margin of safety. That is why the TE budget, not wA* = 228%, sets the size.
