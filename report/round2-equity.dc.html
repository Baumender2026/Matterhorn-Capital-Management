1 · Investment case
Fair value vs price

The two-stage DDM gives USD 28.06, against a price of USD 21.28. At a cost of equity of 6.66%, the price implies only 1.1% dividend growth forever. Yet consensus has adjusted EPS rising from 1.37 to 1.57 by next fiscal year, and the company targets 5–7% operating profit growth [3]. GAAP hides this: trailing EPS of 0.62 carries charges such as a USD 56m loss on the Brazil sale [1].

Margin of safety and alpha

Margin of safety +31.8%, alpha +34.0% a year (IR 1.47), assuming the gap closes within one year.

Catalyst

Q4 results, FY26 accounts and FY27 guidance, the first from the new CEO · About 3 Dec (Yahoo). Reported on 4 Dec in 2024 and 2025 [4]; this year's date is not yet confirmed. The main event: guidance on algorithm supports EPS of 1.57, and FY26 cash flow could clear the FCF red flags.

61st annual dividend increase · Late Nov (expected). Last year's came on 24 Nov 2025 [2]. Not yet announced. A raise above last year's 1% would signal that the CEO expects cash flow to recover.

Decision

Long candidate. A low-beta, conservatively financed company yielding 5.5%, with dated news inside the challenge. The comparables give USD 17.58, so we read the margin as an upper bound.

2 · Valuation: dividend discount model
Why this method

Hormel has paid a dividend every quarter since 1928 and raised it 60 years running [2], with no buybacks in FY24–FY25, so dividends are the cash owners receive. We use two stages because earnings are below normal: higher growth while margins recover, then a low terminal rate.

Methods that do not fit

Gordon alone would ignore the recovery or extend it forever. Trailing P/E is distorted by one-off charges. P/B says little after write-downs across the sector. A cash-flow DCF would start from FY25 FCF, the lowest in four years (USD 534m).

1 · Investment case
Fair value vs price

The two-stage DDM gives USD 67.23, against a price of USD 94.08. At a cost of equity of 6.68%, the price implies 4.4% dividend growth forever, against about 4% at the last raise [2]. The business is doing well: Q3 volume rose 5% and organic revenue 7% [1]. But the market has paid for this, at 26.7x forward earnings against 17.6x for peers.

Margin of safety and alpha

Margin of safety −28.5%, alpha −1.4% a year (IR −0.10), assuming the gap never closes.

Catalyst

Sale of 75% of Coca-Cola Beverages Africa closes · By 31 Dec (company). Guidance assumes a Q4 close, subject to approvals [1]; South Africa cleared it in September [3]. It makes the 2027 drag concrete, about 8% off revenue and 2% off EPS [1], but this is already guided: a small move.

Eleventh Circuit ruling on the IRS tax case · No date set. Heard on 25 Jun 2026, still pending in the Q3 10-Q [4]. It can come any day, but there is no deadline. It cuts both ways: a full loss costs up to about USD 22bn, near USD 5 a share; a win returns USD 6.6bn [4].

Decision

No position: negative alpha. This is an excellent company at a full price, not a mispricing we can buy. Alpha is negative even if the gap never closes, so we would not hold it in a long-only book.

2 · Valuation: dividend discount model
Why this method

Coca-Cola has raised its dividend for 64 years [2] and returns cash mainly through it: USD 6.8bn of dividends against USD 0.9bn of buybacks in nine months [4]. We use two stages because earnings are growing faster than a global staple can sustain forever: 6.0% for 5 years, then 3.0%.

Methods that do not fit

Gordon alone would apply one rate to both phases. A cash-flow DCF would start from FY25 FCF, cut by the one-off fairlife payment [5]. P/B says little: brands built in-house are not on the balance sheet. Few peers have these margins, so the comparables are only a double check.
