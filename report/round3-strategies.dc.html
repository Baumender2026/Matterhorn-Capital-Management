The factor and why it pays

DRAFT · Owner: Filippo Selmi (with Aaron Arauz) · A5 draft of pages 3–6 (quant 01–02 re-run on 28 Nov, 27 Nov export). Check: (1) the team's reason for BABQM over MCM Momentun (page 6); (2) the "why now" view, then copy it into quant 02 §1d; (3) after Saturday's chain, the 27 Nov screen (page 4) and wQ (quant 02 §7); (4) whether EQBT returns are net of costs and include dividends; (5) the numbers against quant 02 §3–§5.
CP1.1 · The factor: low beta, quality and momentum

BABQM is a defensive multi-factor strategy: every month it buys the top 2% of the Russell 1000, about 30 stocks, on a score that combines low beta, quality and momentum. Low beta is the core: low-beta stocks have earned about as much as high-beta stocks, with less risk. Quality and momentum remove the low-beta stocks that are calm for a bad reason, weak profits or a falling price (the “defensive trap”). Low risk with momentum and shareholder yield is the Conservative Formula (Blitz and van Vliet, 2018); low beta with quality is the basis of defensive-equity funds (e.g. AQR). In our backtest (Nov 2006 – Oct 2026), adding them raised the low-beta rank's Sharpe ratio from 0.46 to 0.56 and its beta from 0.54 to 0.68.

Low beta · the coreEQY_BETAadjusted beta against the S&P 500

Evidence. The security market line is too flat (Frazzini and Pedersen, 2014); low volatility works too (Blitz and van Vliet, 2007).

Why it pays. Investors who cannot borrow buy high-beta stocks instead, so these become too expensive; benchmarked managers avoid low-beta ones.

QualityRETURN_ON_ASSET (LTM)net income / average total assets, LTM

Evidence. Profitable, safe firms earn higher risk-adjusted returns (Asness, Frazzini and Pedersen, 2019; Novy-Marx, 2013).

Why it pays. Investors prefer exciting stories to dull, profitable firms.

Its job. It drops low-beta firms calm only because the business is weak.

MomentumTOTAL_RETURN (1Y, 1M)12-1 month: (1 + R12m) / (1 + R1m) − 1

Evidence. Jegadeesh and Titman (1993); in Class 6 the Mom factor has a Sharpe ratio of 0.46 since 1927 and an alpha of 9.9% (t = 6.4).

Why it pays. Investors react slowly to news; then others join the trend.

Its job. It keeps out the defensive stocks whose price is already falling.

CP1.1 · Risk or behaviour?

Mostly behaviour and constraints: low-beta and profitable firms are less risky, not more, so a risk story is weak. The low-beta premium comes from leverage aversion and benchmarking, quality from neglect, momentum from slow reaction and then herding; the one risk channel is funding, to which Frazzini and Pedersen link the BAB return. The answer matters for persistence (Class 6): a behavioural premium lasts while the constraint or the mistake lasts and is hard to trade away. Leverage limits and benchmarked mandates are structural, so we expect the premium to persist, though it can be crowded.

CP1.1 · Why now: from the 27 November close to 27 December

Our market outlook of 26 November reads stress: the VIX at 27.5 (up 9.1 points in a month), the S&P 500 down 6.4% over the month and the US high-yield spread at 369 bp (41 bp wider). Futures price a 98.6% probability of a 25 bp Fed hike on 9 December, and the outlook's takeaway is to avoid adding risk before the FOMC. The round asks for an equity factor sleeve, and in this month we prefer a defensive one: BABQM's beta was 0.68 (Nov 2006 – Oct 2026), and in the months when the Russell 1000 fell it lost 2.5% on average, against 3.9% for the index. We do not expect it to beat the market in four weeks; we expect it to hold up better if the stress continues.

CP1.1 · The risks
  • A sharp rebound. With a beta of 0.68, BABQM lags rallies (April–May 2026: −2.8% against +15.7%). We do not hold the short leg behind momentum crashes (April 2009: −34.4%, Class 6), but we would lag such a rebound.
  • Rising long-term yields. Low-beta sectors behave like bonds: two of the six worst months, January 2022 and December 2024, saw sharply rising long yields (page 6). A hawkish dot plot on 9 December could repeat it.
  • Funding and crowding. BAB suffers when funding tightens (Frazzini and Pedersen, 2014); a crowded factor falls when many funds sell at once (Class 6, August 2007).
  • Concentration. On 27 November, 9 of the 29 stocks are energy companies, so the sleeve also carries an oil bet: Brent fell 18% in the month to 26 November.

CP1.2 · Long-only

The academic BAB factor buys low-beta stocks with leverage and shorts high-beta stocks, so that its beta is zero. We cannot short or borrow: we hold only the stocks with the best scores, and the others are underweight relative to the market, never short. The sleeve keeps a beta below 1 (0.68 in the backtest): a defensive equity position with a factor tilt, not a market-neutral factor. The list turns over by 26.7% a month on average (Nov 2006 – Oct 2026); in the challenge we trade it once and hold it.

We halve the full-sample Sharpe ratio, the Class 4 rule of thumb. The raw 0.56 overstates what we expect: we chose among 9 trials (with no true alpha, about 1 in 20 looks significant at 5%), all run on the full 2006–2026 sample, so the validation years were visible when we chose. The macro strategy: regimes

DRAFT · Owner: Tommaso Rotondò (with Elena Rocco) · After Saturday's chain: fill wM and the TE (macro 02 §5; rehearsal 20.0%, capped, TE 5.14%) and re-check every 03b number on pages 7–8; decide on 03b's SAA row (0.589 is over T1's 291 months; over T2's 294 it is 0.636 in our re-run); confirm LEGATRUH (DES), the CFNAI date (21 Dec) and, after B5, the outlook numbers.
2.1 · Our choice

We implement Strategy A because it turns an observable macro view into a single liquid position with dated exit events. The view is stagflation: growth below trend (CFNAI 3-month average −0.05) and inflation rising (CPI 3.7% y/y against 3.0% a year earlier). The position is hedged global bonds, which lower the portfolio's risk in a month in which our outlook shows elevated stress (VIX 27.5 on 26 November, up 9.1 points in a month). We leave Strategy B out: country value and momentum did not beat an equal-weighted basket of the same 21 countries (Sharpe 0.44 against 0.45; mean information coefficient −0.004), and its top pick, Korea, would add a second semiconductor bet next to NVDA. The whole macro sleeve is in BNDX, the rule's single pick; splitting it with Strategy B would add the equity risk our regime view tells us to avoid. Neither candidate beat its benchmark in the backtest: Strategy A earned a Sharpe ratio of 0.51 against 0.59 for our SAA (0.25 after the ×0.5 discount), and the TA's own country strategy did not beat its benchmark either (0.37 against 0.46).

2.2 · Why it should pay, 27 November to 27 December

In the 86 stagflation months since 1991, BNDX had the best Sharpe ratio of our eight asset classes (0.75; BND 0.71), and our three equity ETFs had negative ones (VTI −0.06, VEA −0.48, IEMG −0.42). Our outlook of 26 November adds three reasons. Stress: VIX 27.48 (+9.09 in a month), S&P 500 −6.4%, US high-yield spread 369 bp (+41 bp). Funded pro rata from the SAA, the sleeve swaps a slice that is 31% equities and 24% gold and commodities for investment-grade bonds. The dollar: DXY +1.74% in a month and +4.61% in three; BNDX is hedged into dollars, VEA and IEMG are not. The Fed: a 25 bp hike on 9 December is 98.6% priced, so the risk is in the dots and in CPI the next day; BNDX holds no dollar bonds, so US rates reach it only through global yields. Hedging adds roughly the gap between US and foreign short rates (Fed 4.00%; ECB 2.50%, BoJ 1.25%), and the hike widens it, but US cash earns the same rate: it raises BNDX's return, not its excess return. We expect the sleeve to beat the SAA slice it replaces if the stress lasts into the FOMC and CPI, and to lag it if it fades.

2.3 · Risk premium or behavioural bias?

Mostly a risk premium, plus a timing claim we cannot prove. Over cash, BNDX earns the term premium of high-grade bonds outside the US, the reward for bearing the risk that yields rise. The rule's timing holds bonds instead of equities when growth is weak and inflation rising, when equities lost on average. A behavioural reading (prices slow to absorb data that arrive late and are revised) is possible but untested. Against the timing claim, BNDX's Sharpe ratio is positive in all four regimes (0.47 to 0.98) over decades of falling yields: part of the pick is that bond rally, not the regime. Its risks: an equity rally and rising global yields (CP2.3, page 8), and thin evidence (2.4).

The view is an environment, not one event: growth below trend while prices rise. In our sample, equities and commodities lost in those months and high-grade bonds held up. Our outlook of 26 November argues for less risk this month: stress (VIX 27.48, S&P 500 −6.4%), a Fed hike to 4.25% priced for 9 December, and a rising dollar (DXY +1.74%) that the hedge keeps out.

CP2.2 · The instrument: BNDX

Vanguard Total International Bond ETF (BNDX US Equity, USD): investment-grade bonds outside the US, currencies hedged into dollars (Bloomberg Global Aggregate ex-USD Float Adjusted RIC Capped Index; duration about 6 years). (1) It is the rule's pick, and we do not override it: BND (0.71) is a statistical tie, and a choice by hand would be a trial the backtest never ran. (2) The hedge keeps the dollar out, so the position is a bet on foreign bond yields, not on currencies; unhedged IGOV would add currency risk (Round 1). (3) It is long-only and already in our SAA (16.48%), chosen under our liquidity rule (assets of at least USD 1 billion): one row in PRTU, "SAA + Macro".

CP2.3 · If the regime changes or the catalyst fails

The sleeve lags the SAA if equities rally, and loses money if global yields rise. The first fits a regime change: equities led in Overheating months (IEMG 0.73, VTI 0.63; BNDX 0.47) and VTI in Slowdown months (1.20), and the market doubts the inflation leg (5-year breakeven −31 bp to 2.15%), as do economists (ECFC US CPI 3.45% in 2026, 2.58% in 2027). On the second, the hedge removes currency risk, not rate risk (BNDX's correlation with BND: 0.89 in 2021–2026, Round 1), and 5 of 14 central banks in our outlook hiked in the past month. If the stress fades, the sleeve forgoes a month of equity premium.
