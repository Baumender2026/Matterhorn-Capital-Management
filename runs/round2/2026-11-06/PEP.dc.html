1 · Investment case
Fair value vs price

The Gordon model gives USD 160.10, against a price of USD 141.34. At a cost of equity of 7.20%, the price implies only 2.5% growth forever, below the 3.1% organic revenue growth of Q3 [1]. The market has priced the cut in 2026 EPS guidance [1], not the cost programme agreed with Elliott, which targets at least 1 point of margin by 2028 [2].

Margin of safety and alpha

Margin of safety +13.3%, alpha +14.2% a year (IR 0.93), assuming the gap closes within one year.

Catalyst

Update on the North America supply chain and go-to-market · Late 2026 (company). Promised to analysts and investors for late 2026 in the agreement with Elliott [2]; no date set yet. Figures on an integrated food and drinks network and its savings would make the margin target concrete.

New structural cost actions · Coming months (company). Announced with Q3: actions are being identified and will be implemented in the coming months [1]. Savings that fund price investment and volume would lift the earnings path the market discounts.

Decision

Long candidate. We buy, although the margin of +13% is below our 25% rule: the comparables (USD 167.55) are above the price too. A defensive stock with dated news from management inside the challenge.

2 · Valuation: dividend discount model
Why this method

PepsiCo is mature and pays out most of its earnings: USD 8.9bn of cash returns planned for 2026, of which USD 7.9bn in dividends [1]. Bloomberg's long-term growth of 3.4% is close to the long-run rate, so one stage is enough: the Gordon model with growth of 3.0%.

Methods that do not fit

Two stages add little when g1 is this close to g2. P/B (8.65x) says little: the brands were built in-house and are not on the balance sheet, and leverage shrinks the equity. Coca-Cola sold its bottlers and has other margins, so the comparables are only a double check.

6 · Specific issues
Main risk: leverage and the payout

With dividends and buybacks at 1.13 times FCF and high debt, a further profit cut would put pressure on buybacks first. North American drinks volume fell 3% in Q3 [1]. If the cost plan slips, the market may keep pricing growth near 2.5%.

Upside: an activist and management quality

Elliott took a stake of about USD 4bn in 2025 [3]. The agreement brought a supply-chain review, nearly 20% fewer US products and board refreshment [2]. CEO Ramon Laguarta must now deliver the margin target, under activist scrutiny.

Model flags: IR of 0.93 is above Buffett's 0.64: the alpha is probably too optimistic.
